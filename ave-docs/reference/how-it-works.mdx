## Sign-in

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your app sends the user to Ave">
    Your app opens `https://aveid.net/signin` with its `client_id`, `redirect_uri`, scopes, PKCE challenge, `state`, and `nonce`, either by redirecting or through the embed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The user authenticates and consents">
    The user signs in with a passkey, picks an identity, and approves the scopes your app asked for.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ave redirects back">
    Ave returns to your `redirect_uri` with `?code=...&state=...`. If your app asked for an encryption scope, the keys follow in the fragment: `#app_key=...`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your app exchanges the code">
    Your app sends the code and PKCE verifier to `POST https://api.aveid.net/api/oauth/token` and receives an `id_token`, access tokens, and, with `offline_access`, a refresh token.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your app creates its session">
    Verify the `id_token`, key your records by its `sub`, and keep the encryption key on the client.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Encryption keys

Every Ave account has a master key. It is generated on the user's device and stored on Ave only in encrypted form, unlocked with passkey PRF output, a recovery code, or a transfer from another signed-in device. The master key never leaves the device in plaintext.

When an app first asks for `e2ee:symmetric` or `e2ee:asymmetric`, the Ave sign-in page generates the app's key material in the browser, encrypts it with the master key, and stores only that ciphertext on the authorization. On every later sign-in, the page decrypts the stored ciphertext locally and places the plaintext in the callback fragment. Fragments are not sent in HTTP requests, so the plaintext reaches your client code without passing through Ave's API or your server.

Keys are stable per app and identity. A user who signs in again, or on another device, gets the same key. A different identity gets different keys. Rotation only happens when your app asks for it with `e2ee:reset`.

See [Encryption model and key lifecycle](/security/encryption-model-and-key-lifecycle) for details.

## Responsibilities

| Layer | Operated by | Responsible for |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-in pages and accounts | Ave | Passkey authentication, identity selection, consent, key wrapping and unwrapping on the user's device |
| Ave API | Ave | Token issuance, userinfo, storing wrapped keys, public-key lookup |
| Your client | You | Encrypting and decrypting user data, keeping keys out of logs and URLs |
| Your backend | You | Verifying tokens, storing ciphertext, workspaces, membership, and permissions |

<Note>
  Ave tells you who the user is and delivers their keys. What that identity is allowed to do in your app is decided by your app. See [App workspaces and permissions](/guides/app-authorization).
</Note>
