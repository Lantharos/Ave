---
title: "App workspaces and permissions"
description: "Use Ave identity with workspaces and authorization managed by your app."
---

Ave authenticates an identity. Your app decides what that identity can access.

Verify the ID token's signature, issuer, audience, expiry, and nonce before creating an app session. Use the verified `sub` as the stable identity ID. Display names, handles, and email addresses can change and should not be used as authorization keys.

## Store membership in your app

Maintain your own workspace and membership records:

```ts
type Membership = {
  workspaceId: string;
  identityId: string;
  role: "owner" | "admin" | "member";
};
```

For each protected request, authenticate the app session, load the membership for the requested workspace and verified identity, then check the role required by the operation. A workspace ID supplied by the browser is a selection, not proof of membership.

Keep billing, invitations, project permissions, and membership changes in your app. Revoking membership should take effect in your app immediately, independently of the lifetime of an Ave token.

## Choose the identity boundary

One Ave account may contain multiple identities. Each identity has a different `sub`. Treat these as separate app principals unless your product explicitly supports account linking. Require authenticated proof before linking identities; do not merge accounts based on matching email or display name.

## Encrypted workspaces

With `e2ee:asymmetric`, a workspace can have its own symmetric key that only members hold:

1. The member who creates the workspace generates a random AES-256 workspace key on their device.
2. For each member, wrap the workspace key with `encryptForAppUser(workspaceKey, member.publicKey)` and store the envelope with the membership row.
3. A member unwraps their envelope with `decryptFromAppSender(envelope, session.getAppPrivateKeyBase64()!)` and uses the workspace key for workspace data.
4. To add someone, an existing member wraps the key for the new member's public key. To remove someone, rotate the workspace key and re-wrap it for the remaining members.

Your server stores envelopes and ciphertext, and enforces membership on every request, but never holds a workspace key. See [End-to-end encryption](/guides/end-to-end-encryption) for the SDK calls.

## OAuth scopes

Scopes authorize your app to obtain specific Ave information or capabilities. For example, `email` requests a verified email address and encryption scopes deliver app keys. These scopes do not grant workspace membership or product permissions.

## Developer teams

Teams in the [developer portal](https://devs.aveid.net) own your Ave apps and their credentials. They are separate from your product's workspaces. Add members by the verified email address on their Ave identity. Every member can manage the team's apps, client secrets, members, and team profile, and a team always keeps at least one member. Each app in the portal lists the identities that have authorized it.
